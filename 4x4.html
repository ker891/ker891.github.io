The 4x4 Cube Solver

WARNING! THIS TUTORIAL REQUIRES KNOWING HOW TO SOLVE A 3x3!

Introduction

The 4x4 was invented in 1981 almost a full decade after the original Rubik's Cube was made
The 4x4 provides a far more challenging puzzle with 7.4 quattuordecillion (74 followed by 45 zeroes) combinations. However, the current 4x4 record in 15.18 seconds!
Here I will be guiding on how to solve the 4x4 Rubik's Cube!

First, I will you assume you know the 3x3 and it's pieces, well the same applies here, but instead of edges we have wings and we have no single center but seperate center pieces. There technically is a true center, but we're going to skip over it in this tutorial.
Below is the notation sheet as there are 3 new algorithms to learn for this. However, most of beginner 4x4 is intuitive, meaning that there is no specific algorithm for everything and you will have to learn solve cases outside of the examples here.

Making Centers

First, we're going to make our own centers since there isn't a center. Like usual we're going to start with the white. Find two white center and try to line them up into bar.
Next find the other two white centers and make them into a bar as well then pair upn the two bars to make the white center the move the it to the bottom.

Next is the yellow center. Remember that yellow is opposite white on a normal 3x3 and as such must also be opposite when making centers. First make another bar of yellow centers and move it up. You should notice that this ruins the white center so move the U layer twice so that none of the yellow bar is in the layer that you just moved to move it up top and move the white bar back down. Use this same method to take a yellow center out of the top
Then make the second yellow bar and align it with the yellow bar that already on top so that when you move the second bar up it move the first bar. Then, move the second bar out of the layer that you just moved and then return the layer to how it was.

That's two of six centers done!

Next is the rest of the centers and most of it is making bars, matching them up and making centers without breaking the other centers. Tere are many cases of these that you simply have to learn intuitively.
Remember to keep the color scheme of regular Rubik's Cubes with white on the left and yellow on the right going up is: Blue, Orange, Green, Red A useful way to remember this is through the acronym BOGR.
